Experience

Government management and coordination

My time in the public sector combined technical analysis with the political coordination of government action. I worked at the core of presidential agenda tracking, first at the Ministry General Secretariat of the Presidency and then at the Ministry of the Interior, where the task was to turn the Executive's priorities into verifiable commitments and make sure they actually moved forward, bringing together ministries, agencies and regional authorities.

Adviser, Interministerial Coordination Division (DCI)

Ministry of the Interior · Nov. 2025 – Mar. 2026

The Interministerial Coordination Division coordinates and monitors the fulfilment of presidential commitments across the State. Its role is to ensure that decisions taken at the highest level of government are not diluted across ministries, but have owners, deadlines and results that are systematically monitored.

Within the Division I was the sector lead for two ministries, Finance and Culture, Arts and Heritage. That meant knowing their agenda in detail, keeping a direct line with their teams and following the progress of more than 330 presidential commitments at the national level. The work consisted of gathering information, verifying the real status of each commitment, anticipating bottlenecks and producing the inputs that allowed the centre of government to decide where to focus.

I also had a territorial responsibility, tracking more than 170 regional commitments and investment projects in the Maule and Ñuble regions. This meant bringing the national view down to the regional level, coordinating monitoring with the Regional and Provincial Presidential Delegations and working directly with regional ministerial secretaries and agency heads to unblock projects and keep government priorities aligned on the ground.

In that role I coordinated the Investment Monitoring Committees (CSI) of both regions, which bring regional authorities together around the progress of public investment. I convened presidential delegates, regional secretaries and agency heads, led the closing of the cycle and consolidated the information into final regional reports. The common thread was combining technical and political criteria. It is not enough to know whether a commitment is moving; one has to understand why it is stuck and which decision will move it.

Professional internship, Ministry General Secretariat of the Presidency (SEGPRES)

Ministry General Secretariat of the Presidency · Aug. 2024 – Dec. 2024

My first experience in tracking the government agenda was at SEGPRES, the ministry in charge of the Executive's political coordination and its relationship with Congress. There I tracked legislative progress and built policy baselines, producing inputs for decision-making at the centre of government.

Much of the work was analytical, organising and systematising programme information from national plans with an emphasis on verifiable results, and helping to update databases and procedure manuals that improved the traceability of institutional work. It was also my first contact with high-level coordination meetings, including meetings at La Moneda Palace, where I saw at first hand how the government's direction is put together.
