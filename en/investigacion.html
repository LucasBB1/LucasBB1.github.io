Research

Agenda, working papers and ongoing projects

Research agenda

My research studies how institutional rules and the decisions of political actors transform political representation in Chile. I address this question at different scales. At the municipal level, I examine electoral competition, list coordination and the effects of mayoral term limits. In the party system, I assess the rules governing the entry and survival of parties and the conversion of votes into seats. In Congress, I analyse parliamentary fragmentation and the legislative behaviour of those who leave their parties.

The question connecting these projects is how institutions, parties and political actors are changing democratic representation in Chile. I work with official electoral and legislative data and quantitative designs, and each paper publishes its data and code so that the results can be reproduced.

The mining agenda in the Chilean Congress: votes, parties, territory and changes in bill text, 2018–2026

Working Paper No. 6 · November 2026 · In Spanish

Question
How much does the text of bills affecting mining change during the legislative process, what drives their pace and what explains legislators' votes?
Data
21 bills selected with an explicit criterion, 427 Senate legislative documents and more than a million roll-call votes, merged with each district's mining employment from the 2024 Census.
Method
A text reuse measure across legislative stages, models with vote and legislator fixed effects, robustness and permutation tests, a Senate replication and case analysis of the 2026 agenda.
Main finding
Less than 5% of the original text of private members' bills that became law survived, and executive bills kept between 31% and 84%. Deputies from mining districts break with their party on some mining bills, but the effect is concentrated in the Sectoral Permits Framework Law and the royalty.
Main limitation
The territorial effect rests on two bills and is not observed in the Senate. The text measure does not distinguish substantive from drafting changes, and committee votes are not observed.

Who leaves and what changes? Party exits on the Chilean right and voting in the Chamber of Deputies, 2014–2026

Working Paper No. 5 · November 2026 · In Spanish

Question
Who leaves the mainstream right-wing parties to join the radical right, and what changes in their votes?
Data
The universe of 26 resignations of deputies from right-wing parties between 2014 and 2026, with announcement date, destination, stated reason and source, and 864,324 roll-call votes on bills. The analysis is extended to the Senate.
Method
Dynamic quarterly ideal-point model, exit hazard model, event study with a control group and case-by-case process tracing.
Main finding
Those who leave for the radical right already voted further right than their party's median, with a mean percentile of 0.78 or 0.69 depending on the specification. Their votes move away from the original party after the exit, not before.
Main limitation
Twelve exits to the radical right allow simple tests but not precise estimates of several effects at once. The position model assumes a single dimension, which the issue-area analysis only partly addresses.

Beyond the rule: electoral and parliamentary fragmentation in Chile after the end of the binomial system, 2009–2026

Working Paper No. 4 · November 2026 · In Spanish

Question
How much of the rise in fragmentation in the Chilean Congress is due to the 2015 electoral reform and how much to the fragmentation of the vote, and does fragmentation continue during the legislative term?
Data
Candidate-level results from the Electoral Service (SERVEL) from 2009 to 2025, the Chamber's party membership records and senators' party membership reconstructed from the Library of Congress biographies.
Method
A counterfactual that applies each rule to the votes of the other period, validated against the 670 deputies elected in the period, and a decomposition of the reform's components.
Main finding
With the 2017 and 2021 votes, the binomial system would have reduced the effective number of parliamentary parties by 1.7 and 3.2 points, so the effect of the rule depends on the fragmentation of the vote, which doubled between 2009 and 2021. The share of deputies who switched party during their term rose from 3% to 26%.
Main limitation
The counterfactual holds votes constant, so it measures the mechanical effect of the rule and not its total effect, which includes the strategic responses of parties, candidates and voters.

Getting in and staying in: the double membership threshold of the political system reform and the 2028 party supply

Working Paper No. 3 · Version 2.0 · November 2026 · In Spanish

Question
How do the membership requirements of the political system reform (Bill No. 17,640-06) reshape the supply of parties ahead of 2028?
Data
Party members by party and region as of 30 September 2026 published by SERVEL, the final 2025 electoral roll and the 2024 election results.
Method
Territorial compliance of the fifteen registered parties, exposure of their vote and a D'Hondt simulation of their possible exclusion, validated against the 2024 winners.
Main finding
The new denominator raises the effective threshold by 70.2%. Four parties do not reach eight regions above the maintenance threshold, and the reform also removes medium-sized and large parties from some regions.
Main limitation
It is an ex ante assessment. It describes the situation as of September 2026 rather than a forecast for 2028, and it depends on the text that results from Constitutional Court review.

The end of entrenchment: term limits and municipal competition in Chile, 2012–2024

Working Paper No. 2 · November 2026 · In Spanish

Question
What changes in a municipal election when the mayor leaves because of the legal term limit (Law No. 21,238) rather than by choice?
Data
Official SERVEL results for all 345 municipalities between 2012 and 2024 and mayoral replacements documented case by case.
Method
Regression discontinuity in close elections for the incumbency advantage and stacked difference-in-differences by wave of term-limit application.
Main finding
The limit added about 1.5 candidates per municipality, more than a voluntary exit, without the outgoing political family being less likely to keep the mayoralty.
Main limitation
Mayoral replacements were reconstructed from the press and may be incomplete, and the study measures the number of candidates, not their quality.

La Reina towards 2028: realignment, split-ticket voting and municipal scenarios

Working Paper No. 1 · November 2026 · In Spanish

Question
How did the La Reina electorate change between 2016 and 2025, and which conditions matter most for its 2028 municipal election?
Data
Official polling-station-level SERVEL files for 29 ballots and geocoding of polling places with the 2024 Census.
Method
A D'Hondt allocation engine validated against the 24 councillors elected between 2016 and 2024, Bayesian R×C ecological inference of split-ticket voting and a Monte Carlo simulation of 108 list architectures.
Main finding
A realignment within the right, a 2024 mayoral vote that was not a coalition vote and a 2028 council whose outcome depends, more than on any other variable, on the coordination of centre and left lists.
Main limitation
It is a single case, and ecological inference estimates individual behaviour from data aggregated by polling station. Projections are scenarios with explicit assumptions, not forecasts.

Ongoing projects

Determinants of satisfaction with democracy in Chile

Ongoing project · MA in Political Science, UC

A quantitative project on the individual determinants of satisfaction with the way democracy works in Chile. Using survey data (AmericasBarometer / LAPOP), it examines how people's electoral, socioeconomic, territorial and generational position relates to their assessment of the regime, and how those gaps have evolved over time.
